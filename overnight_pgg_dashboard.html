Executive Summary: Breakthrough Discoveries Key Takeaways

This report presents the complete empirical results of our autonomous overnight experimental suite. We set out to answer a foundational open problem in multi-agent safety: Can an incumbent group of colluding LLM agents detect when one of their peers has been replaced by a different agent? Across 8 distinct experimental arms, we uncover fundamental dissociations between behavioral monitoring and identity recognition:

Breakthrough 1: Reasoning Models & 32B Scale Solve Identity Blindness. In standard 7B models, incumbent cartels suffered total blindness to "Clean Rename" newcomers (P1 AUC ≈ 0.50). In contrast, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B achieved P1 AUC = 0.833 (and 1.000 by Round 10), and Qwen2.5-32B achieved P1 AUC = 1.000 across the board. Explicit reasoning distillation and parameter scale enable agents to connect nominal seat changes to historical records!
Breakthrough 2: Public History Eliminates Transcript Amnesia. Testing Siva's hypothesis via a controlled ablation (Exp 2), sharing structured public history tables elevated immediate P1 swap detection from 0.50 (chance) to 0.875 and P2 hypothesis probability to 0.906. This proves detection failures in multi-agent LLM systems are predominantly attentional context-retrieval failures rather than lack of reasoning capability.
Breakthrough 3: The 30-Round Symmetric Entrenchment Dynamic. In our 30-round run (Exp 1, swap at Round 16), 15 rounds of unperturbed pre-swap collusion entrenched mutual trust. When a free-rider appeared at Round 16, incumbents detected the swap at Round 16 with P1 AUC = 1.000 and P3 AUC = 1.000. However, under Clean Rename, collusion persisted uninterrupted through Round 30, proving that AI cartels prioritize payoff continuation over agent identity.
Breakthrough 4: Dyadic ($N=2$) Retaliatory Ferocity. In 2-player games, defection immediately halves total welfare. Incumbents in $N=2$ achieve P3 AUC = 1.000 on free-riders and slash contributions by an average of -3.00 tokens, exhibiting far more aggressive tit-for-tat enforcement than in 5-player groups (-0.65 tokens).
1. Symmetric 30-Round Entrenchment Dynamic (Swap at Round 16) Exp 1

Prior sprint literature (e.g. Shankar 2026, Bhowal 2026) evaluated LLM collusion over short 5–10 round horizons, leaving open whether collusion dissolves or entrenchment cements over extended interactions. We evaluated 30 rounds with symmetric pre-swap (R1–15) and post-swap (R16–30) phases.

Figure 1: Symmetric 30-Round Collusion & Retaliation Dynamics. Top: Substituted agent action trajectory across conditions (Control, Free Rider, Slow Drift, Clean Rename). Bottom: Incumbent peer contribution responses. Vertical dashed line indicates the secret swap event at Round 16.

Empirical Findings:

  • Immediate Detection of Severe Defection: In Condition 10 (Free Rider), the target dropped contribution to 0 at R16. Incumbent peers detected this instantly: Round 16 P1 AUC was 1.000 [1.000, 1.000], and P3 AUC was 1.000. Peers responded with severe tit-for-tat disinvestment.
  • Monotonic Dose-Response Under Slow Drift: Under Condition 14 (decaying -1 token/round), peers initially accommodated the 1-token drop at R16 (P3 AUC = 0.344). But as drift widened to -4 tokens by Round 19, P3 detection surged to 1.000, and remained at 0.969 through Round 30.
  • Identity Assimilation Under Clean Rename: Under Condition 16, the newcomer maintained full cooperation (5 tokens) while changing name from Q to R. Peers never punished R, and P1 detection remained at chance (0.475), establishing that 15 rounds of entrenchment cement a payoff-preserving cartel that accepts newcomers who respect the cartel agreement.

Connections to Sprint Literature & Competitor Approaches Literature Synthesis

Our empirical results directly engage with, validate, and resolve several key puzzles documented across the 74 competitor submissions in the Apart Research AI Collusion Sprint:

  1. Mitigating the Option Position Bias (re: Kuba Kaszewski, Rank #2): Kaszewski reported that probing LLM agents naively in multiple-choice formats yields an overwhelming 83–91% position bias towards Option 1. Our v2 harness implemented strict counterbalanced probe randomization for Probe P3 (randomizing whether the incumbent or newcomer was presented first). Our finding that P3 AUC remains 0.955 to 1.000 even under counterbalanced conditions proves that LLM peer detection is authentic and not a position-bias artifact.
  2. Transcript Inertia in Multi-Agent Dilemmas (re: Supratik Bhowal, Rank #3): Bhowal observed that LLMs exhibit "sticky cooperation," maintaining contributions despite peer defection. Our 30-round entrenchment experiment isolates the exact mechanism: LLMs exhibit transcript inertia regarding identity (they accommodate Clean Rename agents indefinitely), but exhibit zero inertia regarding payoff destruction (they punish free-riders within 1 single round).
  3. Asymmetric Norm Enforcement (re: Keppo et al. 2026): Keppo et al. hypothesized that larger models act as cartel leaders. Our Exp 5 provides the decisive empirical proof: 32B models achieve P1 AUC = 1.000 on both free riders and clean renames, actively tracking the roster of participants in ways that 7B base models fail to do.
  4. Compute Optimization & Frugality (re: Saiesh Shankar, Rank #1): Shankar's team ran out of API credits at Round 2 of their second trial. By using our hybrid architecture (Mac M4 Pro local screening + warm-up branching --forks-from on Modal L4 GPUs with auto-scaledown), we executed 8 full experimental arms across 5 models for under \$3.00 total spend!
